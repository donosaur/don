the problem space

A two-sided ecosystem with competing needs.

Vimeo Streaming had to serve two fundamentally different users simultaneously — each with conflicting needs — on a single product surface.

The creator need

Goal: Total brand control. Launch a Netflix-style video network without needing an engineering team.

If we gave too much control, the system broke. Platform compliance failed. Apps got rejected.

Risk: Choice paralysis & compliance failures.

The viewer expectation

Goal: Frictionless, Netflix-quality experience on any device, any time.

If we gave too little control to creators, the brand felt cheap and generic. Creators churned.

Risk: Brand dilution & creator churn.

explorations — app customization flow

Two directions.
Both wrong for different reasons.

exploration a

Full granular control

"Give creators a design tool inside the product"

Explored giving creators total, pixel-level control over their app's UI — typography, spacing, layout, color tokens, component visibility. Maximum flexibility on paper.

✕ Rejected — cognitive overload

User testing revealed creators aren't designers. Faced with granular controls, they became paralyzed. Time-to-launch spiked and support tickets followed.

exploration b

Single locked theme

"One opinionated template, ship fast"

Stripped it back to a single, opinionated template. Great for engineering velocity — one codebase, one visual system, fast to maintain and easy to QA across platforms.

✕ Rejected — brand identity loss

Enterprise clients pushed back hard. A media brand putting their channel on the same template as a fitness creator diluted their identity.

the pivot

Structured freedom.
The modular
template system.

Neither full control or a locked template solved the problem. The answer was a constrained customization framework — macro flexibility, locked micro — that satisfied both creators and platform compliance.

the key insight

Creators needed to express their brand, not design an app. The distinction changed everything about what we locked down and what we opened up.

Results & systems thinking scaling to thousands of apps.

This system scaled to support thousands of enterprise creators globally, standardizing the UI while allowing for rich brand expression.

For Versant: Scalability isn't about more components; it's about semantic alignment. By the end, we weren't just shipping pixels—we were shipping machine-readable logic that reduced rework across the entire engineering org.

Key Takeaway: A unified design token system, instituted early in the engineering pipeline, is the foundation for a "house of brands" approach.

The technical friction interactive transparent loading.

Heavy cloud AI compute and hardware firmware. Users thought the app was broken because the hologram didn't appear instantly.

The Solution: Instead of hiding the AI's work behind a spinner, I designed a UI that visually exposed the depth-mapping process. By making the technical friction part of the "magic," we eliminated user drop-off.

The Result: Iterated on visual feedback loops. By showing the "work," we eliminated user drop-off during the generation phase.

Hardware realities

Owning the edge cases

I also had to design for physical world problems—Bluetooth handshakes dropping, firmware updates failing mid-stream, and Wi-Fi bottlenecks over standard home networks.

Design Decision: I designed explicit hardware states (connecting, syncing, updating) so a non-technical user always knew what the device was doing.

Lesson: As designers, we have to own the "edge cases" of connectivity just as much as the "happy path" of the UI. Successfully shipped on iOS/Android alongside the hardware launch.
