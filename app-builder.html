Explorations — App Customization Flow

Two directions.
Both wrong for different reasons.

Exploration A

Full granular control

"Give creators a design tool inside the product"

Explored giving creators total, pixel-level control over their app's UI — typography, spacing, layout, color tokens, component visibility. Maximum flexibility on paper.

✕
Rejected — Cognitive overload User testing revealed creators aren't designers. Faced with granular controls, they became paralyzed. Time-to-launch spiked and support tickets followed. Power without guardrails isn't a feature.

Exploration B

Single locked theme

"One opinionated template, ship fast"

🔒
LOCKED TEMPLATE

Stripped it back to a single, opinionated template. Great for engineering velocity — one codebase, one visual system, fast to maintain and easy to QA across platforms.

◐
Rejected — Brand identity loss Enterprise clients pushed back hard. A media brand putting their channel on the same template as a fitness creator diluted their identity. We were asking them to pay for a product that looked like everyone else's.

The Pivot

Structured freedom.
The modular template
system.

Neither full control nor a locked template solved the problem. The answer was a constrained customization framework — macro flexibility, locked micro — that satisfied both creators and platform compliance.

The key insight

Creators needed to express their brand, not design an app. The distinction changed everything about what we locked down and what we opened up.

Honest Retrospective

If I could go back —

↑
I'd have pushed for a unified design token system earlier Maintaining visual parity across 5+ platforms without a shared token layer created compounding UI debt. Every brand color change became a 5-platform update. It was avoidable.
↑
I'd have run enterprise user testing before Exploration B We discovered the brand identity problem from client pushback, not from research. A single round of interviews with enterprise clients would have killed Direction B at the sketch stage.
→
The modular system worked — but accrued complexity over time Every new creator tier requested new customization options. The framework held, but maintaining the "guided options" layer became its own design and engineering tax.
→
Platform compliance locked us in more than we anticipated What we locked for Apple and Roku compliance in 2017 was still locked in 2024 — even when the guidelines changed. Compliance debt is real and harder to unwind than technical debt.
